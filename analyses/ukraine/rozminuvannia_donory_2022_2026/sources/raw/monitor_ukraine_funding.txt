 Ukraine # 

 In 2024, 22 donors contributed a total of US$252.7 million to mine action activities in Ukraine ( see table below ). This represented 34% of all international donor funds, although it was a decrease of 18% from the $308.1 million received in 2023.
 The largest contribution to Ukraine was from the European Union (EU), which provided $55.8 million to support capacity-building, survey and clearance, including mine detection dogs, and risk education. This represented 83% of the EU’s total contribution for the year. The second and third largest donors—Switzerland and Germany respectively—provided a combined total of $50.4 million. Austria contributed $5.4 million to Ukraine in 2024, comprising almost its entire contribution, with funds allocated for clearance, risk education, and victim assistance activities implemented by the United Nations (UN) World Food Programme (WFP) and Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). [1] All the funds provided by Jersey, $0.7 million, were allocated to explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) training and victim assistance in Ukraine through Jersey Overseas Aid. United States (US) funding to Ukraine decreased by about $70 million from its 2023 contribution of $90 million. [2] 
 Of the total contribution to Ukraine in 2024, a total of $182.9 million (72%) went toward clearance activities, while $45.3 million (18%) went to capacity-building and $6.9 million (3%) went to dedicated risk education and victim assistance activities. The remining $17.6 million (7%) went to mine action activities that were not disaggregated by donors.

 Funding developments # 
 In 2024, the United Kingdom (UK), Netherlands and Switzerland funded a United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) feasibility study into enhancing finance for mine action in Ukraine, which identified two models—an agriculture bond (linked to future profit) and a public private partnership (businesses fund demining in exchange for concessions and land ownership)—as potential options for Ukraine. The recommendations were under consideration by the Ukrainian authorities. [4] 
 Ukraine has been the one of the top recipients of US funding for mine action since 2022. The US funding freeze imposed in January 2025 impacted mine action operations; however, as of the end of July 2025, only one project grant—focused on capacity-building—had been terminated among 10 international mine action operators. [5] 
 National contributions # 
 Ukraine has not disclosed its financial contributions to the state institutions responsible for humanitarian demining activities but reported that about 3 billion Ukrainian hryvnia (UAH) (approximately $70.9 million) was budgeted for demining agricultural land in 2024, with 80% provided by the state and 20% by farmers. [6] 
 Five-year support for mine action # 
 In the five-year period from 2020–2024, international contributions to mine action in Ukraine totaled approximately $758.9 million. The significant rise in funding since 2022 reflects a shift in donor funding, with many donors directing funds to Ukraine following Russia’s full-scale invasion of the country in February 2022. Ukraine was the largest recipient of international funding in the period 2020–2024. [7]
